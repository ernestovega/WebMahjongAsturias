Learn Mahjong

Start with the basic rules. Then choose MCR or Riichi and practice with exercises and apps.

Start here: 5 steps

  1. The tiles. There are three numbered suits from 1 to 9, the winds and the dragons. MCR also uses 8 flowers. MCR has 144 tiles. Riichi has 136.
  2. The goal. Build a hand of 14 tiles. The hand has four sets and one pair.
  3. The sets. A chow is three consecutive numbers of the same suit. A pong is three identical tiles. A kong is four identical tiles.
  4. The turn. You draw a tile. Then you discard a tile. You can also take another player's discard if it completes a set.
  5. Winning and scoring. You win when you complete the hand. The points depend on the variant.

Duplicate format

The Duplicate format reduces the effect of luck. The tables play the same deals. The result of each player is compared with the results of the others. It works like duplicate Bridge.

You play it online on MahjongSoft (opens in a new tab). It is the only platform that supports it. Read more on the Duplicate page.

Other rules

There are many more Mahjong rules. These are four of the most widespread.

  • SBR (Sichuan Bloody Rules). It comes from Sichuan, in China. It uses 108 tiles, with no honours and no flowers. Each player chooses a suit that they will not use and must discard it first. You cannot take discards to make chows. The hand does not end when someone wins. It continues until only one player has not won.
  • American. It is played mostly in the United States. It uses 152 tiles, with 8 jokers. The valid hands are on a card that the National Mahjong League publishes each year. At the start, the players pass tiles between them. This phase is called the Charleston. The book “Mahjong Americano: Paso a paso” (opens in a new tab), by Héctor Escaso, a co-founder of Mahjong Madrid, explains the game. The book is only in Spanish. In Spain, see the Instagram of Mahjong Americano Madrid (opens in a new tab).
  • Taiwanese. It is played in Taiwan. Each player has 16 tiles instead of 13. The winning hand has five sets and a pair. It uses flowers and counts points in tai.
  • Hong Kong. It is played in Hong Kong. It uses 144 tiles, with flowers. The winning hand has four sets and a pair. Points are counted in fan.
